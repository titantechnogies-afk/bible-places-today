@Lev
The burnt offering: an unblemished animal wholly given to God, accepted on the worshipper's behalf. Jesus offered himself wholly to the Father, and we are accepted in him (Ephesians 1:6).
The grain offering, with no leaven but with salt and oil. It pictures Jesus' perfect, sinless life, the bread of life anointed by the Spirit.
The peace offering, shared as a meal with God. Jesus made peace through the blood of his cross (Colossians 1:20); we now feast with God in fellowship.
The sin offering for unintentional sin. Even sins people did not know about needed covering. Jesus' one sacrifice covers all sin, known and unknown (Hebrews 10:14).
The guilt offering and confession. Under the law, sin had to be dealt with again and again. In Christ, God remembers our sins no more (Hebrews 10:17).
Restitution and the fire on the altar that never goes out. The continual fire pictures a sacrifice always available; Jesus' sacrifice is eternally effective.
More on guilt and peace offerings; the priests eat their portion. Under the new covenant all believers are priests who share in Christ's sacrifice.
Aaron and his sons are washed, clothed and anointed. Believers are washed by the Word, clothed in Christ's righteousness and anointed by the Spirit (1 John 2:20).
The priests begin their ministry; fire from the Lord consumes the offering and the people shout for joy. God accepted the sacrifice, just as he accepted Jesus' sacrifice by raising him.
Nadab and Abihu offer unauthorised fire and die. The old covenant showed the danger of approaching God on our own terms. Through Jesus we now come boldly, by his blood (Hebrews 10:19).
Clean and unclean animals. Jesus declared all foods clean (Mark 7:19), and Peter's vision showed that God now makes all people clean through Christ (Acts 10:15).
Purification after childbirth. Mary offered two birds, the offering of the poor (Luke 2:24): Jesus was born into humble circumstances to make us rich (2 Corinthians 8:9).
Laws about skin diseases and examination by the priest. Lepers were outcasts, yet Jesus touched them and made them clean (Mark 1:41).
The cleansing of a healed leper with two birds: one killed, one released. A picture of Jesus' death and resurrection: by his death we are set free.
Bodily discharges and uncleanness. Under the law uncleanness spread by touch; with Jesus, his cleanness spread to the woman who touched him (Mark 5:25-34).
The Day of Atonement: the high priest enters the Most Holy Place and the scapegoat carries sins away. It had to be repeated yearly; Jesus entered once for all with his own blood (Hebrews 9:12).
The life is in the blood, and blood makes atonement (17:11). That is why Jesus' blood is so precious: his life was given for ours.
Laws on sexual relationships. God's boundaries protect life and family. In Christ we are new creations empowered by grace to live free (Titus 2:11-12).
"Be holy, for I am holy" and "love your neighbour as yourself" (19:2, 18). Jesus fulfilled this law of love, and his love is now poured into our hearts (Romans 5:5).
Penalties for serious sins under the law. The law brought death; the Spirit gives life (2 Corinthians 3:6). Jesus took the penalty we deserved.
Holiness of the priests. Jesus is our perfect High Priest, holy, innocent and undefiled (Hebrews 7:26), and he makes us holy.
Rules for the priests and acceptable offerings without blemish. Jesus was the lamb without blemish or spot (1 Peter 1:19).
The feasts of the Lord: Passover, Firstfruits, Pentecost, Trumpets, Atonement and Tabernacles. Jesus fulfilled Passover (his death), Firstfruits (his resurrection) and Pentecost (the Spirit).
The lampstand, the showbread and a case of blasphemy. Jesus is the light of the world and the bread of life who sustains us.
The sabbath year and the Jubilee: debts cancelled, slaves freed, land restored. Jesus announced the ultimate Jubilee: freedom for captives (Luke 4:18-19).
Blessings for obedience and discipline for disobedience under the old covenant. Yet even here God says he will remember his covenant (26:42). In Christ we receive the blessings because he obeyed perfectly.
Vows and dedications and how to redeem them. Jesus redeemed us, not with silver or gold but with his precious blood (1 Peter 1:18-19).
@Num
The census of Israel's fighting men. God knows his people by name, and in Christ every believer is counted and known.
The arrangement of the camp around the tabernacle. God placed himself at the centre of his people; Jesus is in the midst of those gathered in his name (Matthew 18:20).
The Levites are taken in place of the firstborn. A picture of substitution: Jesus, God's firstborn, took our place.
The duties of the Levite clans in carrying the tabernacle. Every member has a part; in the body of Christ each one is needed (1 Corinthians 12).
Purity in the camp and the test for suspected unfaithfulness. Under the law, suspicion and bitter water; in Christ, the cup of blessing and trust.
The Nazirite vow and the priestly blessing: "The Lord bless you and keep you; the Lord make his face shine on you" (6:24-26). In Jesus, God's face shines on us with favour.
The leaders bring generous offerings for the altar. God records every gift; nothing given to him in love is forgotten.
The lampstand is set up and the Levites are dedicated. The light shines forward, a picture of Jesus lighting our way.
The second Passover and the cloud that led Israel. When the cloud moved, they moved. God leads his people; the Holy Spirit now guides us (Romans 8:14).
The silver trumpets, and Israel sets out from Sinai. The trumpets gathered the people; one day the trumpet will sound and we will be gathered to Jesus (1 Thessalonians 4:16).
The people complain about manna, and God gives quail and his Spirit to seventy elders. Moses wished all God's people had the Spirit (11:29); at Pentecost that came true.
Miriam and Aaron criticise Moses, and Miriam is struck with leprosy. Moses prays for her healing. Love prays for those who wrong us.
The twelve spies explore Canaan; ten bring a bad report and two believe. Unbelief, not giants, kept them out. Faith sees God's promise bigger than the obstacles.
The people refuse to enter and want to return to Egypt. Moses intercedes and God forgives (14:20). They missed the promise through unbelief (Hebrews 3:19); rest is entered by faith.
Offerings for when they enter the land, and the tassels as reminders. God still spoke of "when you come into the land": his promises outlast our failures.
Korah's rebellion. Leadership is God's choice, not something seized. Aaron's censer stopped the plague, picturing Jesus standing between death and life.
Aaron's dead staff buds, blossoms and bears almonds overnight. A picture of resurrection: Jesus, our priest, lives by the power of an indestructible life (Hebrews 7:16).
Duties of priests and Levites; God is their inheritance. In Christ, the Lord himself is our portion and inheritance.
The red heifer, whose ashes cleansed from contact with death. Hebrews 9:13-14 says Christ's blood does far more: it cleanses our conscience from dead works.
Moses strikes the rock instead of speaking to it. The rock was struck once (Exodus 17); Christ was crucified once. Now we only need to speak and receive.
The bronze serpent: whoever looked at it lived. Jesus said he would be lifted up the same way so whoever believes in him has eternal life (John 3:14-15).
Balak hires Balaam to curse Israel, and Balaam's donkey speaks. God protects his people from curses they do not even know about.
Balaam tries to curse but can only bless: "God is not a man that he should lie" (23:19). What God has blessed no one can curse.
Balaam prophesies a star from Jacob and a sceptre from Israel (24:17). A prophecy of Jesus, the bright Morning Star (Revelation 22:16).
Israel sins with Moab at Peor and a plague follows; Phinehas stops it. Where enemies could not curse God's people, temptation was used instead. Grace keeps us.
A second census of the new generation. A new generation, a new beginning: God's purposes continue.
The daughters of Zelophehad ask for an inheritance, and God says yes. God's heart is generous and he hears those who ask boldly. Joshua is appointed to lead.
Daily and sabbath offerings. These continual sacrifices pointed to Jesus' once-for-all sacrifice that never needs repeating.
Offerings for the feasts. Every feast pointed to Christ, in whom all things are fulfilled.
Vows and promises. God is faithful to every promise he makes; all his promises are "Yes" in Christ (2 Corinthians 1:20).
War against Midian. A hard chapter of old covenant judgement; under the new covenant our battle is not against flesh and blood (Ephesians 6:12).
Reuben and Gad settle east of the Jordan. God honours requests, but there is a lesson about settling short of the fullness God has for us.
The stages of Israel's journey from Egypt to Jordan. God remembered every step; he is faithful through the whole journey of our lives.
The borders of the promised land. God's promises are specific and generous.
Cities of refuge where the person who killed accidentally could flee and be safe. Jesus is our refuge; in him we are safe from condemnation (Hebrews 6:18).
Inheritance kept within the tribe. God protects the inheritance of his people. In Christ our inheritance is kept in heaven and cannot fade (1 Peter 1:4).
@Deu
Moses recounts the journey from Sinai and the failure at Kadesh. God carried them "as a man carries his son" (1:31) even when they did not trust him.
The wilderness years. "These forty years the Lord your God has been with you; you have lacked nothing" (2:7). God's provision is faithful even in the wilderness.
Victory over Og and Moses' request to enter the land. Moses saw the land but could not enter: the law could not bring people into the rest; Joshua (Jesus) did.
Moses urges Israel to keep the law, and warns that idolatry would scatter them, yet promises that if they seek God they will find him (4:29). God is merciful and will not forget his covenant (4:31).
The Ten Commandments repeated. The people wanted Moses to stand between them and God; Jesus is now the one mediator who brings us near (1 Timothy 2:5).
The Shema: "Love the Lord your God with all your heart" (6:5). No one could keep this perfectly except Jesus, who now pours God's love into our hearts so we love because he first loved us.
God chose Israel not because they were many or good but because he loved them (7:7-8). Grace has always been the reason for God's choice.
Man does not live by bread alone but by every word from God's mouth (8:3). Jesus quoted this in the wilderness. God humbles and feeds us to show his faithfulness.
Not because of your righteousness (9:5-6). Moses reminds Israel of the golden calf. God's blessing is not a reward for goodness, a principle fulfilled in grace.
God loves the stranger, the orphan and the widow. Circumcise your heart (10:16). In Christ, God does this for us (Colossians 2:11).
Blessing and curse set before Israel under the old covenant. Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law by becoming a curse for us (Galatians 3:13).
Worship at the place God chooses. Jesus said true worshippers will worship in spirit and truth, not tied to one place (John 4:21-24).
Warnings against false prophets who lead people away from God. Test every teaching by Jesus and his finished work.
Clean and unclean foods and the tithe that was eaten in celebration. The tithe in Deuteronomy 14 was a feast of joy before God; God loves cheerful giving.
The year of release: debts cancelled and slaves freed. A picture of the gospel, where our debt is cancelled (Colossians 2:14).
Passover, Pentecost and Tabernacles. Jesus fulfilled these feasts: our Passover Lamb, the giver of the Spirit, and God dwelling with us.
Justice and the future king, who was to read the law daily. Jesus is the true King, who fulfilled the law perfectly on our behalf.
God promises a prophet like Moses whom the people must hear (18:15). Peter says this is Jesus (Acts 3:22).
Cities of refuge and fair witnesses. Jesus is our refuge, and the Spirit is our witness that we are God's children (Romans 8:16).
Laws for war: "Do not fear, for the Lord your God goes with you to fight for you" (20:4). The battle belongs to the Lord.
Anyone hanged on a tree is under God's curse (21:23). Paul quotes this: Christ became a curse for us on the tree to redeem us (Galatians 3:13).
Various laws about care for neighbours, animals and property. God's law showed care even for birds and lost property; his heart is kind.
Who may enter the assembly. Under the law many were excluded; in Christ, outsiders, foreigners and eunuchs are welcomed (Isaiah 56; Acts 8).
Laws about divorce, loans and fair wages. God's heart for the vulnerable runs through the law and is fulfilled in Jesus' compassion.
Fair weights and remembering Amalek. Justice matters to God; Jesus fulfilled all righteousness for us.
The firstfruits offering and the confession "My father was a wandering Aramean". Thanksgiving for God's deliverance; Israel is called God's treasured possession (26:18), as believers are now (1 Peter 2:9).
Curses pronounced on Mount Ebal. "Cursed is everyone who does not continue in all the law" (Galatians 3:10 quoting 27:26). No one could; Jesus bore the curse for us.
Blessings and curses of the old covenant. The curses included every sickness and poverty. Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law so the blessing of Abraham is ours by faith (Galatians 3:13-14).
The covenant renewed in Moab. "The secret things belong to the Lord, but the things revealed belong to us" (29:29). God has revealed his heart fully in Jesus.
Return and restoration promised; the word is near you, in your mouth and heart (30:14). Paul applies this to the gospel: confess and believe (Romans 10:6-10).
Joshua is commissioned: "He will never leave you nor forsake you" (31:6). Hebrews 13:5 applies this promise to every believer.
The song of Moses about God's faithfulness and Israel's unfaithfulness. "He is the Rock, his work is perfect" (32:4). God remains faithful.
Moses blesses the tribes: "The eternal God is your refuge, and underneath are the everlasting arms" (33:27).
Moses dies on Mount Nebo, seeing the land but not entering. The law (Moses) could not bring God's people into rest; Joshua, whose name is the same as Jesus, would.
