@Gen
God creates everything by his word and calls it very good. Humanity is made in his image, blessed and given dominion. Life began with God's blessing, not our effort; in Christ, the Word through whom all was made, we are made new (John 1:3; 2 Corinthians 5:17).
God plants a garden, forms Adam and makes Eve as his partner. God provided everything before Adam did anything. Rest and provision come first from God; that is the pattern of grace.
The serpent deceives, Adam and Eve sin, and fear and shame enter. Yet God comes looking for them, promises a Seed who will crush the serpent (3:15) and clothes them himself. The first gospel was preached the day sin entered.
Cain kills Abel, yet God puts a mark of protection on Cain. Abel's blood cried out for justice; Jesus' blood speaks a better word: mercy and forgiveness (Hebrews 12:24).
The family line from Adam to Noah; Enoch walks with God and is taken. God's story keeps moving toward the promised Seed. Enoch shows that walking with God is fellowship, not a performance.
Wickedness fills the earth, but Noah finds grace in the eyes of the Lord (6:8). This is the first time "grace" appears in Scripture: Noah was saved by grace, then built by faith (Hebrews 11:7).
The flood comes and Noah's family is safe inside the ark. The ark pictures Jesus: one door, one way of salvation, and everyone inside is kept safe by God, who shut them in (7:16).
The waters recede and Noah worships. God promises never again to curse the ground or destroy the earth by flood. God's promises rest on his faithfulness, not ours.
God makes a covenant with Noah and sets the rainbow as its sign. God swore never to flood the earth again; in Christ he has sworn never to be angry with or rebuke his people (Isaiah 54:9-10).
The nations descend from Noah's sons. God's plan always included every nation; in Jesus the blessing reaches all peoples (Revelation 7:9).
People build Babel to make a name for themselves, and God scatters them. Human effort to reach heaven fails; in Christ, God came down to us, and at Pentecost he reversed Babel by uniting languages in praise (Acts 2).
God calls Abram and promises to bless him and all families of the earth through him. The promise came by God's choice and grace. Galatians 3:8 calls this the gospel preached in advance.
Abram and Lot separate; Abram lets Lot choose first, and God renews his promise. When we trust God's promise we can afford to be generous. God is our portion.
Abram rescues Lot and meets Melchizedek, priest of God Most High, who brings bread and wine. Melchizedek pictures Jesus, our eternal High Priest (Hebrews 7).
Abram believed God and it was counted to him as righteousness (15:6). God alone walks between the pieces, binding himself to the covenant. Righteousness has always been by faith, and the covenant rests on God's oath.
Sarai and Abram try to fulfil God's promise through Hagar. Yet God sees Hagar in the wilderness: El Roi, "the God who sees me". Paul uses this story to contrast law (Hagar) with grace (Sarah) in Galatians 4.
God gives Abraham a new name and the sign of circumcision. God changed his identity before the promise was fulfilled. In Christ we receive a circumcision of the heart, not by hands (Colossians 2:11).
The Lord visits Abraham and promises Isaac; Abraham intercedes for Sodom. Nothing is too hard for the Lord (18:14). Abraham's boldness in prayer shows God's heart to spare, not destroy.
Angels rescue Lot from Sodom before judgement falls. God was merciful to Lot (19:16). Believers are rescued from the wrath to come through Jesus (1 Thessalonians 1:10).
Abraham deceives Abimelech about Sarah, yet God protects them. God's faithfulness to his promise was greater than Abraham's failure (2 Timothy 2:13).
Isaac is born, the child of promise, and Abraham laughs with joy. Paul says believers are children of promise like Isaac, born by the Spirit, not by human effort (Galatians 4:28).
Abraham offers Isaac, and God provides a ram in his place: "The Lord will provide" (22:14). On that same mountain range God would not spare his own Son, but gave him up for us all (Romans 8:32).
Sarah dies and Abraham buys a burial field in Canaan. He owned only a grave in the promised land, yet he died in faith, looking for a city whose builder is God (Hebrews 11:13-16).
Abraham's servant finds Rebekah for Isaac through prayer and God's leading. A picture of the Spirit seeking a bride for the Son: God guides those who trust him.
Abraham dies; Jacob and Esau are born, and Esau sells his birthright for a meal. God's choice of Jacob was by grace, before either had done good or bad (Romans 9:11).
God renews the promise to Isaac. Even when Isaac repeats his father's mistake, God blesses him and his wells overflow. God's blessing rests on his covenant, not on perfect people.
Jacob deceives Isaac and receives the blessing meant for Esau. Jacob grabbed by deceit what God had already promised to give. God's blessing is a gift, and he still works through flawed people.
Jacob dreams of a stairway to heaven with God standing above it, promising to be with him. Jesus said he is that ladder: the way between heaven and earth (John 1:51). God met Jacob at his lowest.
Jacob meets Rachel and is deceived by Laban. God sees Leah, the unloved one, and gives her children; through Leah came Judah and the line of Jesus.
Children are born to Jacob, and he prospers despite Laban's schemes. God was faithful to bless Jacob as he had promised, even in an unfair situation.
God tells Jacob to return home and protects him from Laban. "If the God of my father had not been with me" (31:42). God's presence is our protection.
Jacob, afraid of Esau, wrestles with God and receives a new name, Israel. He came away limping but blessed. God changes our name and nature when we cling to him.
Esau runs to meet Jacob and embraces him. A picture of the Father in Luke 15 who runs to the returning son. Reconciliation is possible because God softens hearts.
Dinah is violated and her brothers take violent revenge. A painful chapter showing human sin at its worst, and why the world needed a Saviour, not just better laws.
Jacob returns to Bethel, puts away idols, and God renews the covenant. Rachel dies giving birth to Benjamin. God keeps calling his people back to himself in grace.
The descendants of Esau. God blessed Esau too and made him a nation. God's goodness reaches beyond the line of promise.
Joseph dreams of greatness, and his brothers sell him into slavery. The beloved son, rejected by his brothers and sold for silver, is a picture of Jesus.
Judah and Tamar: a shameful story. Yet Tamar is named in Jesus' genealogy (Matthew 1:3). Grace writes broken people into God's story.
Joseph prospers in Potiphar's house, resists temptation and is falsely imprisoned. "The Lord was with Joseph" (39:2, 21). God's presence was with him in the pit and the prison.
Joseph interprets dreams in prison and is forgotten by the cupbearer. People may forget you; God does not. Waiting is not wasted when God is with you.
Joseph interprets Pharaoh's dreams and is raised to rule Egypt. From prison to palace in a day. Jesus too was raised from the lowest place to the highest, to save many (Philippians 2:9).
The brothers come to Egypt for grain, and Joseph recognises them. The one they rejected now holds their life in his hands, and he weeps for them.
The brothers return with Benjamin and eat at Joseph's table. Joseph shows them undeserved kindness before they understand who he is: grace before repentance.
Joseph tests his brothers with the silver cup, and Judah offers himself in Benjamin's place. Judah, ancestor of Jesus, becomes a substitute for his brother.
Joseph reveals himself: "God sent me before you to preserve life" (45:5). He forgives completely. A beautiful picture of Jesus, who forgives the brothers who rejected him.
God tells Jacob not to fear going down to Egypt, for he will go with him. Wherever God leads, his presence goes too.
Joseph provides for his family and all Egypt through the famine. The rejected son becomes the saviour of the world, a picture of Jesus as the bread of life.
Jacob blesses Joseph's sons, crossing his hands to bless the younger. God's blessing does not follow human order; grace often chooses the unexpected.
Jacob blesses his sons and prophesies that the sceptre will not depart from Judah until Shiloh comes (49:10). Jesus is the Lion of the tribe of Judah (Revelation 5:5).
Joseph forgives his brothers again: "You meant evil against me, but God meant it for good" (50:20). God turns even betrayal into salvation, as he did at the cross.
@Exo
Israel multiplies in Egypt and is enslaved; the midwives fear God and protect the babies. God's people were helpless slaves. Salvation would have to come from God, not from themselves.
Moses is born, hidden, rescued and raised in Pharaoh's palace; he flees after killing an Egyptian. God heard their groaning and remembered his covenant (2:24). God's rescue begins with his remembering, not their worthiness.
God meets Moses at the burning bush and reveals his name: I AM. Jesus later said "Before Abraham was, I AM" (John 8:58). The God who sees affliction comes down to deliver.
Moses makes excuses and God answers each one with his presence and power. God does not call the qualified; he qualifies the called. His sufficiency is our confidence.
Pharaoh refuses and makes the work harder. Before deliverance things looked worse. God's timing and promises stand even when circumstances seem to deny them.
God promises: I will bring you out, deliver you, redeem you, take you as my people (6:6-7). Seven "I will"s: salvation is all God's doing.
Aaron's staff becomes a serpent and the Nile turns to blood. God shows his power over Egypt's gods. Jesus' first miracle turned water into wine: life, not death.
Frogs, gnats and flies. God makes a distinction between his people and Egypt (8:23). Those who belong to God are set apart by his grace.
Livestock die, boils and hail. God protects Israel's animals and land. Under the new covenant believers are redeemed from the curse (Galatians 3:13).
Locusts and darkness, yet Israel had light in their homes (10:23). Jesus is the light of the world; his people do not walk in darkness (John 8:12).
The final plague is announced. God makes a clear distinction between his people and Egypt. Salvation will come through what God provides.
The Passover: a spotless lamb, its blood on the doorposts. "When I see the blood, I will pass over you" (12:13). Jesus is our Passover Lamb (1 Corinthians 5:7); safety is in his blood, not our goodness.
The firstborn are consecrated and God leads them by a pillar of cloud and fire. God did not lead them the short way into war; he knows our frame and leads with care.
The Red Sea parts. "The Lord will fight for you; you need only be still" (14:14). The greatest deliverance of the Old Testament was entirely God's work, a picture of salvation.
Israel sings the song of Moses; bitter water is made sweet; God reveals himself as the Lord who heals (15:26). The tree that sweetened the water pictures the cross.
God sends manna and quail even though Israel grumbles. Grace fed complainers. Jesus is the true bread from heaven (John 6:32-35).
Water from the rock, and victory over Amalek as Moses' hands are held up. The rock was Christ (1 Corinthians 10:4); struck once for us, it gives living water.
Jethro advises Moses to share the load. God never meant for one person to carry everything. In the body of Christ we bear one another's burdens.
Israel arrives at Sinai. God reminds them he carried them on eagles' wings, but the people say "All that the Lord has said we will do" (19:8), trusting their own ability. The law was given to show that no one could keep it (Romans 3:20).
The Ten Commandments. God first says "I am the Lord who brought you out" (20:2): rescue before rules. The law is holy, but it was a tutor to bring us to Christ (Galatians 3:24).
Laws for servants and justice. Under the old covenant justice was "eye for eye"; Jesus revealed a better way of mercy (Matthew 5:38-39).
Laws about property and restitution. The law dealt with outward actions; Jesus came to give a new heart.
Laws about justice, mercy for the poor and the sabbath year. God's heart for the poor and stranger shines through even in the law.
The covenant is confirmed with blood. The old covenant was sealed with animal blood; the new covenant is sealed with Jesus' own blood (Hebrews 9:18-24; Luke 22:20).
God asks for a sanctuary so he can dwell among his people: the ark and mercy seat. The mercy seat, where blood was sprinkled, pictures Jesus our propitiation (Romans 3:25).
The tabernacle curtains and coverings. Every detail pointed to Christ, the Word who "tabernacled" among us (John 1:14).
The bronze altar and courtyard. The way to God began at the altar of sacrifice; there is no access without the blood. Jesus is our once-for-all sacrifice.
The priests' garments, with the names of the tribes on the high priest's shoulders and heart. Jesus, our High Priest, carries us on his shoulders and over his heart.
The consecration of priests with blood and oil. Believers are now a royal priesthood (1 Peter 2:9), washed by Jesus' blood and anointed by the Spirit.
The altar of incense and the ransom money. Every person's ransom was the same; in Christ we were all redeemed by the same precious blood (1 Peter 1:18-19).
God fills Bezalel with his Spirit for craftsmanship and gives the sabbath as a sign. Jesus is our sabbath rest (Hebrews 4:9-10).
The golden calf. While God gave the law, the people were already breaking it. Moses intercedes and God relents, a picture of Jesus our intercessor.
God promises "My presence will go with you, and I will give you rest" (33:14). Moses asks to see God's glory. In Jesus we see the glory of God (2 Corinthians 4:6).
God reveals himself as merciful, gracious, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love (34:6). Moses' face shone; Paul says the new covenant has far greater glory (2 Corinthians 3:7-18).
The people give willingly and generously for the tabernacle. Giving from a willing heart is the new covenant pattern (2 Corinthians 9:7).
The skilled workers build with more than enough given. When God's people give freely, there is overflow.
The ark, table and lampstand are made. The lampstand pictures Jesus, the light of the world, and the table his provision.
The altar, the basin and the courtyard. The basin of washing pictures the cleansing of the Word (Ephesians 5:26).
The priestly garments, engraved "Holy to the Lord". In Christ we are made holy, not by our effort but by his work (Hebrews 10:10).
The tabernacle is set up and God's glory fills it. Now God's glory fills his people: believers are the temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Corinthians 6:19).
