God commissions Joshua: "Be strong and courageous… I will be with you wherever you go" (1:9). Joshua's name is the Hebrew form of Jesus; he leads God's people into the rest the law could not give.
Rahab hides the spies and is saved by a scarlet cord. A Gentile, a prostitute, saved by faith (Hebrews 11:31), and later named in Jesus' family line.
Israel crosses the Jordan as the priests' feet touch the water. God goes ahead of his people; faith steps in before the waters part.
Twelve stones are set up as a memorial. Remembering what God has done builds faith for what he will do.
Israel is circumcised, the manna stops, and Joshua meets the Commander of the Lord's army. God himself leads the battle.
The walls of Jericho fall after Israel marches and shouts. By faith the walls fell (Hebrews 11:30). Rahab and her family are saved.
Defeat at Ai because of Achan's hidden sin. Under the old covenant one man's sin brought trouble on all; in Christ, one Man's obedience brings righteousness to all (Romans 5:19).
Ai is taken following God's plan. After failure God gives a second chance; his purposes are not cancelled by our mistakes.
The Gibeonites deceive Israel into a treaty. Israel did not ask the Lord (9:14). Yet God later uses even this covenant, showing his faithfulness.
The sun stands still as God fights for Israel. "The Lord fought for Israel" (10:14). Nothing is impossible with God.
Joshua defeats the northern kings. Every promise God made was being kept, step by step.
A list of the kings defeated. God records his victories; we too can count his faithfulness.
Land still to be taken and the inheritance of the eastern tribes. There is always more of God's promise to possess by faith.
Caleb, at 85, asks for the hill country: "Give me this mountain" (14:12). Faith does not retire; God's strength renews us.
Judah's inheritance. Judah, the tribe of Jesus, receives its portion first.
The inheritance of Joseph's sons. God remembered Joseph's faithfulness to the next generation.
The daughters of Zelophehad receive their inheritance as promised. God keeps his word to those who ask in faith.
The tabernacle is set up at Shiloh, and the remaining land is divided. Joshua asks, "How long will you wait before possessing the land?" (18:3). God's gifts are received by faith.
The other tribes' inheritances, and Joshua receives his last. A servant leader who took his share last, a picture of Jesus.
The cities of refuge are set apart. Jesus is our city of refuge; whoever flees to him is safe.
Not one of God's good promises failed; all came to pass (21:45). God is completely faithful.
The eastern tribes build an altar of witness, and a misunderstanding is resolved peacefully. Seek understanding before judging.
Joshua's farewell: not one word of God's promises has failed (23:14). God's faithfulness is the foundation.
"Choose this day whom you will serve; as for me and my house, we will serve the Lord" (24:15). God gave them a land they did not labour for (24:13): a picture of grace.
@Jdg
Israel fails to drive out the Canaanites. Partial victory left enemies in the land. In Christ we have complete victory (1 Corinthians 15:57).
A new generation forgets the Lord, and the cycle of the judges begins. Yet God raises up deliverers out of pity for their groaning (2:18). His compassion moves him to save.
Othniel, Ehud and Shamgar deliver Israel. God uses unlikely people: a left-handed man and a man with an ox goad.
Deborah and Barak defeat Sisera, and Jael finishes him. God raises up whoever he chooses, women included.
The Song of Deborah. "Let those who love him be like the sun when it rises in its might" (5:31).
Gideon, hiding in a winepress, is called a "mighty man of valour" (6:12). God sees who we are in him before we see it ourselves.
Gideon's army is reduced to 300 so Israel cannot boast. Victory comes from God, not numbers; grace leaves no room for boasting.
Gideon pursues the Midianites but later makes an ephod that becomes a snare. Even heroes are flawed; only Jesus is the perfect deliverer.
Abimelech seizes power by murder and is destroyed. Grabbing power ends in ruin; Jesus humbled himself and was exalted.
Israel cries out and God "could not bear Israel's misery" (10:16). God's heart is moved by our pain.
Jephthah, rejected by his family, becomes a deliverer, but his rash vow brings tragedy. God never asks for human sacrifice; he gave his own Son.
Jephthah and the Ephraimites. Pride and division among God's people bring loss; unity in Christ brings strength.
An angel announces Samson's birth to a barren woman. God's grace brings life where there was none.
Samson's riddle and his marriage to a Philistine. God works his purposes even through a flawed and impulsive man.
Samson's strength defeats the Philistines with a donkey's jawbone. The Spirit of the Lord came on him; our strength is from the Spirit too.
Samson and Delilah. Blinded and captive, he prays one last time and God answers. God's grace reaches us even after our failures.
Micah's idol and hired priest. "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (17:6). Without God's leadership, people make religion in their own image.
The Danites steal Micah's idols and priest. Man-made religion cannot protect anyone.
A Levite's concubine is horribly abused at Gibeah. A dark chapter showing the depth of human sin and our need for a Saviour.
Civil war against Benjamin. Sin destroys families and nations; only God can heal such brokenness.
Wives are found for the surviving Benjamites. The book ends with "everyone did what was right in his own eyes" (21:25). Israel needed a king; Jesus is that King.
@Rut
Naomi loses her husband and sons; Ruth the Moabite refuses to leave her: "Your God will be my God" (1:16). God's grace reaches an outsider who clings to him.
Ruth gleans in Boaz's field and finds favour. Boaz tells his men to leave extra grain for her. Grace is favour we do not earn.
Ruth asks Boaz to spread his wing over her as kinsman-redeemer. Jesus is our Redeemer who covers us with his love.
Boaz redeems Ruth and marries her; their son Obed is the grandfather of David. An outsider is written into the family line of Jesus.
@1Sa
Hannah, barren and mocked, pours out her heart to God and receives Samuel. God hears the prayers of the hurting.
Hannah's song: God raises the poor from the dust (2:8). Eli's sons despise the offerings. God exalts the humble.
God calls Samuel in the night: "Speak, Lord, for your servant is listening" (3:10). God speaks to those who listen, even children.
The ark is captured and Eli dies. Religion without relationship cannot protect anyone; God's presence is not a lucky charm.
The ark among the Philistines: Dagon falls before it. No idol can stand before the presence of God.
The ark returns on a cart pulled by cows. God brings his presence back to his people by his own power.
Samuel leads Israel in repentance and God thunders against the Philistines. "Ebenezer: thus far the Lord has helped us" (7:12).
Israel asks for a king like the other nations. They rejected God as their king, yet he still provided. Jesus is the King they truly needed.
Saul is chosen while searching for lost donkeys. God's plans find us in ordinary moments.
Saul is anointed, and the Spirit comes on him; he is changed into another man (10:6). In Christ we are made new by the Spirit.
Saul rescues Jabesh-gilead and is confirmed as king. God gives victory when his Spirit moves.
Samuel's farewell: "Do not be afraid… the Lord will not forsake his people, for his great name's sake" (12:20-22).
Saul offers the sacrifice himself out of fear. Impatience led him to act outside God's way. God is looking for a man after his own heart.
Jonathan's faith: "Nothing can hinder the Lord from saving by many or by few" (14:6). Saul's rash oath nearly costs his son's life.
Saul spares Agag and the best spoil. "To obey is better than sacrifice" (15:22). Religious show cannot replace a heart that trusts God.
David is anointed: "Man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart" (16:7). God chooses the overlooked shepherd boy.
David defeats Goliath: "The battle is the Lord's" (17:47). Jesus, the Son of David, defeated our giant enemies at the cross.
Jonathan loves David as himself and gives him his robe and sword. A picture of covenant love and of Christ clothing us.
Saul tries to kill David; Michal and Jonathan protect him. God protects those he has chosen.
Jonathan's covenant with David, and the arrows warning him. True friendship reflects God's faithful love.
David eats the holy bread at Nob. Jesus used this story to show that mercy is greater than ritual (Matthew 12:3-4).
David's mighty men gather: the distressed, in debt and discontented (22:2). Jesus gathers the broken and makes them mighty.
David saves Keilah and is protected; Jonathan strengthens him in God (23:16). Friends who point us to God are a gift.
David spares Saul in the cave: "I will not lift my hand against the Lord's anointed". Mercy to an enemy reflects the heart of Jesus.
Abigail turns David from revenge. God sends people to stop us from taking vengeance; vengeance is his.
David spares Saul again and takes his spear and jug. David refused to grasp the kingdom by force; God gave it in his time.
David hides among the Philistines. Even God's chosen can lose heart; God is faithful when we are weary.
Saul consults the medium at Endor. Without God, Saul turns to darkness; turning away from God leaves us empty.
David is sent away from the Philistine army. God protects David from fighting against his own people.
Ziklag is burned and the families are taken. "David strengthened himself in the Lord his God" (30:6), and all is recovered.
Saul and Jonathan die on Mount Gilboa. A tragic end that shows the emptiness of a life apart from God.
@2Sa
David mourns Saul and Jonathan: "How the mighty have fallen". David honours even his enemy; love does not rejoice in another's fall.
David is anointed king over Judah, and war with Saul's house follows. God's promises unfold over time.
Abner defects and is murdered by Joab. David grieves; God's kingdom is not built by violence.
Ish-bosheth is murdered and David punishes the killers. David would not gain the kingdom by evil means.
David becomes king over all Israel and takes Jerusalem. The Son of David, Jesus, reigns forever.
David brings the ark to Jerusalem and dances before the Lord. Uzzah's death shows the old way of approaching God; David's joy shows the heart of worship.
God's covenant with David: a son whose throne will last forever (7:13). Fulfilled in Jesus, the Son of David, whose kingdom has no end (Luke 1:32-33).
David's victories. "The Lord gave victory to David wherever he went" (8:6).
David shows kindness to Mephibosheth for Jonathan's sake and seats him at the king's table. A beautiful picture of grace: accepted for another's sake.
David's kindness to the Ammonites is rejected, leading to war. Rejecting kindness leads to conflict; God's kindness leads to repentance (Romans 2:4).
David sins with Bathsheba and has Uriah killed. Even the man after God's heart fell badly. Only Jesus is sinless.
Nathan confronts David, who confesses: "I have sinned". "The Lord has taken away your sin" (12:13). Forgiveness came immediately when David agreed with God.
Amnon violates Tamar, and Absalom takes revenge. Sin's consequences spread through families; only grace breaks the cycle.
Absalom is brought back but not fully restored. Half-forgiveness leaves wounds; God's forgiveness is complete.
Absalom steals the hearts of Israel and David flees. In rejection David trusts God: "Let him do to me what seems good to him".
Shimei curses David, and David refuses to retaliate. Jesus, when reviled, did not revile in return (1 Peter 2:23).
Ahithophel's advice is defeated by Hushai. God answers prayer by turning the counsel of the wise to foolishness.
Absalom is killed and David weeps: "O Absalom, my son! If only I had died instead of you". The Father's heart toward even a rebellious son; Jesus did die in our place.
David returns to Jerusalem and shows mercy to Shimei and Mephibosheth. The returning king brings mercy.
Sheba's rebellion is put down by a wise woman of Abel. Wisdom brings peace and saves a city.
The famine and the Gibeonites; Rizpah's devotion. A hard story about broken covenants; God values faithfulness.
David's song of deliverance: "The Lord is my rock, my fortress and my deliverer" (22:2).
David's last words and his mighty men. God makes an everlasting covenant (23:5) that rests on his faithfulness.
David's census brings a plague, and he buys the threshing floor where the angel stops. That site became the temple mount: mercy met judgement at the place of sacrifice.
