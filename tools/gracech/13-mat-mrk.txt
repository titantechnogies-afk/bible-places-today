The genealogy of Jesus includes Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and "the wife of Uriah": grace in the family line. Jesus is born: "He will save his people from their sins" (1:21), Immanuel, God with us.
Wise men from the East worship the child; the family flees to Egypt. Gentiles were among the first worshippers; God protected his Son.
John the Baptist prepares the way. At Jesus' baptism the Father says "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased" (3:17), before Jesus had done any ministry. Acceptance comes before performance.
Jesus defeats temptation with the Word, then heals all kinds of sickness among the people (4:23-24). He came to undo the devil's works.
The Beatitudes, and Jesus fulfilling the law. He raises the standard ("be perfect") to show that no one can be righteous by law-keeping; we need his righteousness as a gift (Romans 3:21-22). This is before the cross.
Give, pray and fast in secret. "Your Father knows what you need before you ask" (6:8). Do not worry: seek his kingdom and his righteousness, which is a gift. (6:14-15 was taught under the law; after the cross we forgive because we are forgiven, Ephesians 4:32.)
Ask, seek, knock: "How much more will your Father give good things to those who ask" (7:11). The house built on the rock: hearing and doing his words.
Jesus heals a leper with a touch ("I am willing"), the centurion's servant and Peter's mother-in-law, fulfilling Isaiah 53:4 (8:17). Healing is God's will.
Jesus forgives and heals a paralysed man, calls Matthew the tax collector and eats with sinners: "I came not to call the righteous but sinners" (9:13).
Jesus sends the twelve: "Heal the sick, raise the dead… freely you have received, freely give" (10:8). Not a sparrow falls without the Father.
"Come to me, all who labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (11:28). His yoke is easy because he carries the load.
Jesus heals on the sabbath: "I desire mercy, not sacrifice" (12:7). A bruised reed he will not break (12:20).
Parables of the kingdom: the sower, the seed, the treasure and the pearl. God's Word is the seed; a receptive heart bears fruit.
Jesus feeds 5,000 and walks on water; Peter walks too until he looks at the waves. "Take heart; it is I" (14:27).
Jesus heals the Canaanite woman's daughter: great faith from an outsider. He heals the lame, blind and mute, and feeds 4,000.
Peter confesses: "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God". Jesus will build his church on that revelation.
The transfiguration: Moses and Elijah fade and "they saw no one but Jesus only" (17:8). The law and the prophets point to him.
Jesus welcomes children and tells of the shepherd who leaves 99 to find one lost sheep. The parable of the unforgiving servant: we have been forgiven an unpayable debt.
"With man this is impossible, but with God all things are possible" (19:26). Salvation cannot be earned; it is God's gift.
The labourers in the vineyard all receive the same wage. Grace is not based on how long we have worked. Jesus came to give his life as a ransom for many (20:28).
Jesus enters Jerusalem on a donkey and cleanses the temple. The stone the builders rejected has become the cornerstone.
The wedding feast: the king invites everyone, good and bad. The wedding garment is the righteousness he provides.
Jesus confronts religious hypocrisy: heavy burdens on others' shoulders. He longed to gather Jerusalem like a hen gathers her chicks (23:37).
Jesus speaks about the fall of Jerusalem and his return. In context, "endures to the end" concerns surviving the tribulation of AD 70, not earning salvation.
The parables of the virgins, the talents and the sheep and goats. Use what God has given; whatever you do for the least, you do for Jesus.
The Last Supper: "This is my blood of the covenant, poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins" (26:28). Jesus prays in Gethsemane and is arrested.
Jesus is crucified. "My God, why have you forsaken me?" He was forsaken so we never will be. The veil is torn from top to bottom: the way to God is open.
He is risen! "All authority has been given to me… I am with you always, to the end of the age" (28:18-20). The new covenant has begun.

John baptises Jesus; Jesus heals many, casts out demons and touches a leper: "I am willing; be clean" (1:41).
Jesus forgives and heals a paralysed man lowered through a roof. He came to call sinners, and the sabbath was made for man.
Jesus heals a withered hand and appoints the twelve "to be with him" and to have authority (3:14-15). Relationship first, then ministry.
The parable of the sower: the Word is the seed and our heart is the soil (4:20). Jesus calms the storm: "Why are you afraid? Have you still no faith?"
Jesus frees the man with a legion of demons, heals the woman who touched his garment ("your faith has made you well") and raises Jairus' daughter.
Jesus could do few miracles in Nazareth because of their unbelief (6:5-6). He feeds 5,000 and walks on water; the disciples' hearts were hardened (6:52).
Jesus confronts traditions that nullify God's Word and declares all foods clean (7:19). He heals the Syrophoenician's daughter and a deaf man.
Jesus feeds 4,000 and heals a blind man in two stages. "Do you still not understand?" Keep receiving.
The transfiguration; "All things are possible for one who believes" (9:23). "I believe; help my unbelief". Jesus casts out the spirit.
Jesus welcomes children and says the kingdom must be received like a child. "With God all things are possible". Blind Bartimaeus receives his sight.
Jesus enters Jerusalem. "Have faith in God… whoever says to this mountain, Be removed, and does not doubt… it will be done" (11:22-24).
The greatest commandment is love. The widow gives all she has. God values the heart behind our giving.
Jesus foretells the temple's destruction and his return. "Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not" (13:31).
Jesus is anointed at Bethany, shares the Last Supper ("my blood of the covenant") and prays in Gethsemane: "Abba, Father, all things are possible for you".
Jesus is crucified; the veil is torn and a centurion says "Truly this man was the Son of God" (15:39).
He is risen! Jesus sends his followers: "These signs will follow those who believe… they will lay hands on the sick and they will recover" (16:17-18).
