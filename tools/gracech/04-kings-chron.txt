@1Ki
Solomon is anointed king as David's life ends. The son of David takes the throne, pointing to Jesus, the greater Son of David.
David's charge to Solomon, and the settling of old accounts. A kingdom established; Jesus' kingdom is established in righteousness and peace.
Solomon asks for wisdom and God gives him wisdom, riches and honour. God gives generously to those who ask (James 1:5).
Solomon's wise rule and peace in the land. Every man under his vine and fig tree, a picture of the peace of Christ's kingdom.
Solomon prepares to build the temple. God provides materials through Hiram; his work is resourced by his provision.
The temple is built. God promises to dwell among his people. Now believers are the temple where God lives (2 Corinthians 6:16).
Solomon's palace and the temple furnishings. Beauty and craftsmanship for God's house; Jesus is the true temple (John 2:21).
The ark comes in and God's glory fills the temple. Solomon prays, "Will God really dwell on earth?" (8:27). In Jesus, God did.
God appears to Solomon again with promises and warnings. Under the old covenant blessing depended on obedience; in Christ it depends on his obedience.
The Queen of Sheba visits and is overwhelmed. Jesus said "One greater than Solomon is here" (Matthew 12:42).
Solomon's heart is turned to other gods by his many wives. Even the wisest man fell; wisdom alone cannot save us. We need a new heart.
The kingdom divides under Rehoboam's harsh rule. Harshness divides; Jesus' yoke is easy and his burden light (Matthew 11:30).
A man of God from Judah prophesies, but is deceived by an old prophet. Hold fast to what God has said.
Jeroboam's son dies and Rehoboam's reign fails. Turning from God brings loss; God remains faithful to David's line.
Kings of Judah and Israel. God kept a lamp for David in Jerusalem (15:4): his promise preserved the line to Jesus.
Wicked kings rise and fall in Israel, and Ahab marries Jezebel. Darkness grows, but God is about to send Elijah.
Elijah is fed by ravens and the widow of Zarephath's flour does not run out. God provides in the most unlikely ways.
Elijah on Mount Carmel: fire falls from heaven. "The Lord, he is God!" Then rain after drought. God answers by fire and refreshes with rain.
Elijah, exhausted and afraid, is fed by an angel and hears God's gentle whisper. God meets us in our weakness with rest, food and a still small voice.
Ahab defeats Aram twice by God's help. God showed mercy even to a wicked king.
Ahab and Jezebel take Naboth's vineyard by murder. Ahab humbles himself and God shows mercy (21:29). God responds to humility.
Micaiah prophesies truly and Ahab dies in battle. God's word cannot be overturned.
@2Ki
Elijah calls down fire on soldiers sent to arrest him. Jesus later rebuked his disciples for wanting to do the same (Luke 9:54-56): he came to save, not destroy.
Elijah is taken to heaven and Elisha receives a double portion. The Spirit's power passes on; Jesus said believers would do greater works (John 14:12).
Elisha prophesies water and victory for the kings. God provides abundantly when we make room for his blessing.
The widow's oil fills every jar, the Shunammite's son is raised, and poisoned stew is healed. God's supply flows as long as there are empty vessels.
Naaman the Syrian is healed by dipping in the Jordan seven times. He wanted to pay, but healing was free. Grace cannot be bought.
The axe head floats and blinded enemies are fed, not killed. "Those who are with us are more than those with them" (6:16). Mercy to enemies wins peace.
Four lepers find the enemy camp abandoned and share the good news. We too carry good news too good to keep to ourselves.
The Shunammite's land is restored, and Hazael becomes king. God remembers his people and restores what was lost.
Jehu is anointed and judgement falls on Ahab's house. God's justice comes in the end.
Jehu destroys Baal worship but does not follow God wholeheartedly. Outward zeal is not the same as a heart that knows God.
Joash, a baby, is hidden for six years and then crowned. God preserved David's line in a hidden child, as he did in Bethlehem.
Joash repairs the temple. God's house is restored when his people give willingly.
Elisha's bones raise a dead man to life. God's power is life-giving even after death; Jesus is the resurrection and the life.
Amaziah and Jeroboam II. "The Lord saw the affliction of Israel… and he saved them" (14:26-27). God's mercy saves even the undeserving.
Kings rise and fall quickly in Israel. Without God, leadership is unstable.
Ahaz copies a pagan altar. Mixing religions leads away from God. Jesus is the only way to the Father.
Israel is taken into exile by Assyria. The old covenant ended in exile; the new covenant brings us home to God forever.
Hezekiah trusts the Lord and the Assyrians mock him. Trust in God is the foundation of victory.
Hezekiah spreads the threatening letter before the Lord and an angel destroys the Assyrian army. Bring your problems to God.
Hezekiah is healed and given fifteen more years. God hears prayer and heals; the shadow goes backward as a sign.
Manasseh's great wickedness. Even so, 2 Chronicles 33 records that he repented and God restored him. No one is beyond grace.
Josiah finds the Book of the Law and humbles himself. A tender heart before God's Word brings revival.
Josiah's great reforms and Passover. Yet the nation still fell; outward reform cannot change hearts, only the new birth can.
Jehoiakim and Jehoiachin; Babylon takes Jerusalem. Exile came under the old covenant, yet God was preparing restoration.
Jerusalem falls and the temple burns, but Jehoiachin is released and eats at the king's table (25:29). Even in exile, a glimpse of grace and restoration.
@1Ch
Genealogies from Adam onward. God tracks every name; history is moving toward Jesus.
The line of Judah, including Tamar, Rahab's descendants and David. Grace writes the broken into the line of the Messiah.
David's descendants. God keeps his promise to David from generation to generation.
The prayer of Jabez: "Oh that you would bless me" and God granted his request (4:10). God loves to bless those who ask.
The tribes east of the Jordan cried out to God in battle and he answered "because they trusted in him" (5:20).
The Levites and singers. God appoints worshippers and priests; in Christ all believers are priests and worshippers.
Genealogies of the northern tribes. Every family matters to God.
Benjamin's line, including Saul's family. History is in God's hands.
Those who returned from exile. God brings his people back; no one is forgotten.
Saul dies on Mount Gilboa. A life that started well ended badly; God's kingdom passes to David.
David is made king and takes Jerusalem; his mighty men. God raises up a king after his own heart.
Warriors join David at Ziklag. "Your God helps you" (12:18). God gathers people around his anointed.
Uzzah dies touching the ark. The old covenant shows the danger of approaching God wrongly; Jesus has opened a new and living way.
David's victories over the Philistines. David inquired of the Lord before battle; God leads us when we ask.
The ark is carried properly with joy and music. Worship flows when we come God's way.
David's psalm of thanks: "Give thanks to the Lord, for he is good; his love endures forever" (16:34).
God's covenant with David: "I will establish his throne forever" (17:12). Jesus is that eternal King.
David's victories. God preserved David wherever he went.
The Ammonites humiliate David's messengers. God gives victory to those who trust him.
Victories over the Ammonites and Philistines' giants. Giants fall before God's people.
David's census and the plague; he buys Ornan's threshing floor. Mercy stopped judgement at the place of sacrifice, the future temple site.
David prepares materials for the temple and charges Solomon. One generation prepares for the next to build.
The Levites are organised for service. God gives each one a place in his house.
Priestly divisions. Zechariah, John the Baptist's father, served in one of these divisions (Luke 1:5).
Musicians and singers who prophesied with instruments. Worship and the prophetic go together.
Gatekeepers and treasurers. Every role in God's house matters.
Army and tribal leaders. God orders his people and his kingdom.
David charges Solomon: "Know the God of your father and serve him with a whole heart" (28:9). Relationship comes first.
David and the people give willingly and rejoice: "Everything comes from you, and we have given you only what comes from your hand" (29:14).
@2Ch
Solomon asks for wisdom and God gives him more than he asked. God gives exceedingly abundantly (Ephesians 3:20).
Solomon prepares to build the temple. A house for God's name; God now dwells in his people.
The temple is built on Mount Moriah, where Abraham offered Isaac. The place of sacrifice became the place of God's presence.
The temple furnishings. Each item pictures Christ: the altar, the basin, the lampstand and the bread.
When the singers praise "He is good; his love endures forever", God's glory fills the temple (5:13-14). Praise welcomes God's presence.
Solomon's dedication prayer. God's ear is open to his people; now we come boldly to the throne of grace.
Fire falls and God answers: "If my people… humble themselves and pray" (7:14). Spoken to Israel under the old covenant; in Christ, God has already reconciled us to himself (2 Corinthians 5:19).
Solomon's achievements. God's blessing was on Solomon's kingdom.
The Queen of Sheba visits. "Happy are your servants who stand before you". Jesus is greater than Solomon.
Rehoboam's harsh answer divides the kingdom. Pride divides; humility unites.
Rehoboam strengthens Judah, and many who seek God move to Jerusalem.
Rehoboam abandons God and Egypt invades; he humbles himself and God relents. God responds to humility with mercy.
Abijah trusts God and wins against Jeroboam. "God is with us at our head" (13:12).
Asa relies on God and defeats a huge army. "Lord, there is no one like you to help the powerless" (14:11).
Azariah encourages Asa: "The Lord is with you while you are with him". Revival follows.
Asa relies on Syria instead of God, and does not seek God in his sickness. "The eyes of the Lord run to and fro… to strengthen those whose heart is completely his" (16:9).
Jehoshaphat sends teachers of God's law. Teaching God's Word strengthens a nation.
Jehoshaphat's alliance with Ahab nearly costs his life; he cries out and God helps him (18:31).
Jehoshaphat appoints judges: "With the Lord there is no injustice". God's justice is good.
Facing a vast army, Jehoshaphat prays "Our eyes are on you" and sends singers ahead. "The battle is not yours but God's" (20:15). Praise brought victory.
Jehoram's wickedness. God keeps the lamp of David burning because of his covenant (21:7).
Athaliah tries to destroy the royal line but baby Joash is hidden. God protects the line of the Messiah.
Joash is crowned and Athaliah falls. God's promise prevails over every scheme.
Joash repairs the temple, but turns away after Jehoiada dies. Faith must be personal, not borrowed.
Amaziah: "The Lord is able to give you much more than this" (25:9). Trust God over money.
Uzziah prospers while he seeks God, but pride leads him to burn incense and he becomes leprous. Pride after success is a danger.
Jotham becomes mighty because he walks steadily before God.
Ahaz's wickedness; yet a prophet persuades Israel to release captives from Judah. Mercy among enemies.
Hezekiah opens and cleanses the temple and restores worship. Revival starts when God's house is opened again.
Hezekiah's great Passover: he prays for those not ceremonially clean and God heals the people (30:18-20). Grace triumphs over ritual.
Generous giving for the priests, so much that heaps remain. Revival produces generosity.
Sennacherib threatens, Hezekiah prays, and God sends an angel. "With us is the Lord our God to help us and fight our battles" (32:8).
Manasseh, the most wicked king, humbles himself in captivity and God restores him (33:12-13). No one is beyond God's mercy.
Josiah finds the law, tears his clothes, and leads reform. A tender heart toward God's Word brings blessing.
Josiah's great Passover. Celebration of God's deliverance; Jesus is our Passover.
Jerusalem falls, yet the book ends with Cyrus decreeing that God's house will be rebuilt (36:23). God always has the last word, and it is restoration.
