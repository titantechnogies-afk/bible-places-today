@Hos
Hosea marries Gomer, and their children's names speak of judgement. Yet God promises: "In the place where it was said 'You are not my people', it shall be said 'Children of the living God'" (1:10). Paul applies this to Gentiles (Romans 9:26).
God will allure his unfaithful bride into the wilderness and speak tenderly to her (2:14). "I will betroth you to me forever in righteousness and steadfast love" (2:19).
Hosea buys back his unfaithful wife. A picture of God buying us back at the cost of Jesus' blood.
"My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge" (4:6). Knowing God is life.
God withdraws until they seek his face. He longs for his people to return.
"Come, let us return to the Lord; he has torn us, that he may heal us" (6:1). "I desire steadfast love, not sacrifice" (6:6); Jesus quoted this twice.
Israel is like a half-baked cake and a silly dove. Without God, people turn in every direction.
"They sow the wind and reap the whirlwind" (8:7). Turning from God leads to emptiness.
Israel's joy is taken away by idolatry. God's heart breaks over his people.
"Sow righteousness, reap steadfast love; break up your fallow ground, for it is time to seek the Lord" (10:12).
"When Israel was a child, I loved him, and out of Egypt I called my son" (11:1), fulfilled in Jesus (Matthew 2:15). "How can I give you up?… My compassion grows warm" (11:8).
Jacob wrestled with God and prevailed through tears. God meets those who seek him.
"I will ransom them from the power of the grave… O death, where are your plagues?" (13:14). Paul quotes this about the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:55).
"Return to the Lord… I will heal their apostasy; I will love them freely" (14:1, 4). God's love is free.
@Jol
A locust plague devastates the land. A call to return to God in the face of disaster.
"Rend your hearts and not your garments; return to the Lord, for he is gracious and merciful" (2:13). "I will restore the years the locusts have eaten" (2:25). "I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh" (2:28), fulfilled at Pentecost.
God judges the nations in the valley of decision, but "the Lord is a refuge to his people" (3:16) and dwells in Zion, where the mountains drip with sweet wine. God's final word is restoration.
@Amo
God roars from Zion against the nations for their cruelty. God sees injustice everywhere.
Judgement on Judah and Israel for oppressing the poor. God's heart is for the poor and needy.
"Can two walk together unless they are agreed?" (3:3). God reveals his plans to his servants.
Israel refused to return despite repeated warnings: "Yet you did not return to me". God kept calling.
"Seek me and live" (5:4). "Let justice roll down like waters" (5:24). God wants justice, not empty worship.
Woe to those at ease in Zion who do not grieve for the ruin of their people. Comfort must not make us careless.
Amos intercedes and God relents twice (7:3, 6). God listens to intercession.
A famine of hearing the words of the Lord (8:11). God's Word is our true food.
God will rebuild David's fallen tent (9:11). James quotes this in Acts 15 to show the Gentiles are welcomed by grace. The book ends with restoration.
@Oba
Edom is judged for gloating over Jerusalem's fall, and "the kingdom shall be the Lord's" (verse 21). God defends his people and his kingdom will triumph.
@Jon
Jonah runs from God and is swallowed by a great fish. You cannot outrun God's love.
Jonah prays from the fish: "Salvation belongs to the Lord" (2:9). Jesus said Jonah's three days pictured his death and resurrection (Matthew 12:40).
Jonah preaches, Nineveh repents and God relents. God's mercy is bigger than our expectations.
Jonah is angry that God is "gracious and merciful, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love" (4:2). God cares about 120,000 people who do not know their right hand from their left.
@Mic
Judgement is coming on Samaria and Jerusalem. God's justice is real.
False prophets oppose the truth, but "I will surely gather you… their King passes on before them, the Lord at their head" (2:12-13).
Corrupt leaders and prophets. Micah is filled with the Spirit and power (3:8).
All nations will come to the mountain of the Lord, and swords will become ploughshares (4:1-3).
"But you, Bethlehem… from you shall come forth for me one who is to be ruler in Israel, whose origin is from of old" (5:2). Jesus' birth foretold. "He shall be their peace" (5:5).
"What does the Lord require of you? To do justly, love mercy and walk humbly with your God" (6:8). God wants the heart, not thousands of offerings.
"Who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity? He delights in steadfast love… you will cast all our sins into the depths of the sea" (7:18-19).
@Nam
"The Lord is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble; he knows those who take refuge in him" (1:7). Good news is proclaimed: peace (1:15).
The fall of cruel Nineveh. God's justice comes for oppressors.
Nineveh, the bloody city, is destroyed. God will not let evil reign forever.
@Hab
Habakkuk asks why God allows injustice; God says he is working in ways you would not believe (1:5).
"The righteous shall live by his faith" (2:4), the verse at the heart of the Reformation (Romans 1:17). "The earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord" (2:14).
"Though the fig tree does not blossom… yet I will rejoice in the Lord; I will take joy in the God of my salvation" (3:17-18). Joy in God whatever happens.
@Zep
The day of the Lord is coming. Seek the Lord, all you humble of the land.
Judgement on the nations around Judah. "Seek righteousness, seek humility" (2:3).
"The Lord your God is in your midst, a mighty one who will save; he will rejoice over you with gladness; he will quiet you by his love; he will exult over you with loud singing" (3:17).
@Hag
"Consider your ways". The people built their own houses while God's house lay in ruins. Put God first and he provides.
"The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former… and in this place I will give peace" (2:9). Jesus came to that temple. From this day I will bless you (2:19).
@Zec
"Return to me, and I will return to you" (1:3). God's heart toward his people is jealous love.
Jerusalem will be a city without walls, "for I will be a wall of fire around her and the glory in her midst" (2:5).
Satan accuses Joshua the high priest, but God rebukes him and clothes Joshua in clean garments (3:1-5). A perfect picture of justification: God removes our filthy rags and clothes us in righteousness. "My servant the Branch" is coming.
"Not by might, nor by power, but by my Spirit, says the Lord" (4:6). Grace, grace to the capstone (4:7).
The flying scroll and the woman in the basket. God removes wickedness from his people.
The four chariots and the crowning of Joshua: the Branch will be both priest and king (6:12-13). Fulfilled in Jesus.
God wanted justice and mercy, not ritual fasting. Show kindness and compassion to one another (7:9).
"Old men and women will sit in the streets of Jerusalem… boys and girls playing" (8:4-5). God delights to restore. Many nations will seek the Lord.
"Your king is coming to you, righteous and having salvation, humble and riding on a donkey" (9:9), fulfilled on Palm Sunday. Prisoners of hope will receive double (9:12).
"I will strengthen the house of Judah… I will bring them back because I have compassion on them" (10:6).
The good shepherd is valued at thirty pieces of silver (11:12-13), fulfilled when Judas betrayed Jesus.
"They will look on me, the one they have pierced, and mourn for him" (12:10). The Spirit of grace is poured out.
"On that day a fountain will be opened… to cleanse them from sin and impurity" (13:1). "Strike the shepherd" (13:7), quoted by Jesus before his arrest.
The Lord will be king over all the earth, and his feet will stand on the Mount of Olives. "Holy to the Lord" will be written on everything.
@Mal
"I have loved you," says the Lord (1:2). God's love comes first. The priests offered him blemished sacrifices; Jesus is the perfect offering.
The priests failed to teach truth. God's covenant with Levi was "life and peace". Jesus is our faithful High Priest.
God sends his messenger to prepare the way (3:1), fulfilled in John the Baptist. The tithe passage (3:8-10) was spoken to Israel under the law; Christ redeemed us from its curse (Galatians 3:13). "I the Lord do not change" (3:6).
"For you who fear my name, the Sun of righteousness shall rise with healing in its wings" (4:2). Elijah will come (fulfilled in John the Baptist), and then Jesus.
