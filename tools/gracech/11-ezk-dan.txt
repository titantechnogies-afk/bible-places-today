Ezekiel sees God's glory in Babylon: living creatures, wheels and a rainbow around the throne. God is not limited to the temple; he meets his people in exile.
God sends Ezekiel to a rebellious people and fills him with his Spirit. God equips those he sends.
Ezekiel eats the scroll and it is sweet as honey. God's Word nourishes us.
Ezekiel acts out the siege of Jerusalem. Under the old covenant, sin brought judgement; Jesus bore that judgement for us.
Ezekiel shaves his head as a sign of judgement. The cost of turning away from God was severe.
Judgement on the mountains of idolatry. God wanted his people to know that he is the Lord.
"The end has come". The day of reckoning under the old covenant.
God shows Ezekiel the idolatry in the temple. Religion corrupted from within.
Those who grieve over sin are marked for protection. In Christ we are sealed by the Spirit.
God's glory fills the temple court, then the cherubim prepare to leave. Without God's presence the temple was empty.
"I will give them one heart, and a new spirit I will put within them; I will remove the heart of stone and give them a heart of flesh" (11:19). The new birth promised.
Ezekiel acts out the exile. God's word will be fulfilled.
False prophets who say "peace" when there is no peace, whitewashing a weak wall. God wants truth.
Idols in the heart. God wants to recapture his people's hearts (14:5).
Jerusalem is like a useless vine. Apart from God we can do nothing; Jesus is the true vine (John 15:5).
The parable of the abandoned baby whom God found, washed, clothed and married. God then promises an everlasting covenant and atonement (16:60-63). Pure grace to the undeserving.
The parable of the eagles and the cedar sprig planted by God on a high mountain. Jesus is the tender shoot that becomes a great tree.
"The soul who sins shall die" (18:4, 20). God says "I have no pleasure in the death of anyone; turn and live" (18:32). In Christ we pass from death to life.
A lament for Judah's princes. Leaders who fail; Jesus is the faithful King.
God recounts Israel's history of rebellion, yet he acted "for my name's sake" (20:9). His grace protected his reputation and his people.
The sword of judgement. The crown is removed "until he comes whose right it is" (21:27). Jesus is the rightful King.
God looked for someone to stand in the gap and found none (22:30). Jesus is the one who stood in the gap for us.
The parable of two unfaithful sisters. Unfaithfulness to God brings pain; his faithfulness remains.
The siege of Jerusalem begins and Ezekiel's wife dies. Grief that pictures the loss of the temple.
Judgement on Ammon, Moab, Edom and Philistia for gloating over Judah. God defends his people.
Judgement on Tyre for its pride. Wealth and trade cannot save.
A lament for Tyre, the proud ship that sinks. Everything apart from God passes away.
The king of Tyre, perfect in beauty until iniquity was found in him (28:15), often seen as a picture of Satan's fall.
Judgement on Egypt, the proud crocodile of the Nile. Leaning on Egypt instead of God brings failure.
Egypt's day of doom. Human powers rise and fall.
Pharaoh compared to a great cedar that was cut down. Pride leads to a fall.
A lament for Pharaoh. Death levels the proud.
Ezekiel the watchman. "I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn and live" (33:11). God's heart is life.
God condemns the selfish shepherds and promises: "I myself will be the shepherd of my sheep… I will seek the lost and bind up the injured" (34:15-16). Jesus is the Good Shepherd.
Judgement on Mount Seir (Edom) for its hatred. God defends his people.
"I will sprinkle clean water on you… I will give you a new heart and put my Spirit within you" (36:25-27). This is the new birth in Christ.
The valley of dry bones: "I will put my Spirit within you, and you shall live" (37:14). God brings life where there is only death.
Gog's attack against God's restored people. God protects his people from every enemy.
Gog's defeat. "I will not hide my face anymore, for I will pour out my Spirit" (39:29).
The vision of a new temple. God's plan is to dwell with his people.
The temple's measurements. God's dwelling is perfect and well ordered.
The priests' rooms and the temple's holiness. God sets apart a place for fellowship with him.
God's glory returns to the temple from the east (43:2-5). In Christ God's glory dwells in his people.
The prince and the priests. Only God's appointed may approach; in Christ all believers have access.
The holy district and offerings. God provides for worship in his presence.
Offerings and feasts in the new temple. Worship centred on God's presence.
A river flows from the temple, getting deeper, bringing life wherever it goes (47:9). Jesus said rivers of living water flow from those who believe (John 7:38).
The new city's name: "The Lord is there" (48:35). God's ultimate plan is to live with his people forever.
@Dan
Daniel and his friends refuse the king's food, and God gives them wisdom and favour. God honours those who honour him.
Daniel interprets Nebuchadnezzar's dream of the statue: a stone cut without hands destroys it and fills the earth (2:44-45). Jesus' kingdom will never be destroyed.
Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego in the fiery furnace, and a fourth man "like a son of the gods". God is with his people in the fire.
Nebuchadnezzar's pride and humbling. "Those who walk in pride he is able to humble" (4:37). God restores the humbled king.
The writing on the wall at Belshazzar's feast. "You have been weighed and found wanting". In Christ we are found complete.
Daniel in the lions' den. "My God sent his angel and shut the lions' mouths" (6:22). God protects his faithful.
The vision of four beasts and the Son of Man given an everlasting kingdom (7:13-14). Jesus called himself the Son of Man.
The ram and the goat. God controls history and reveals the future.
Daniel's prayer: "Not because of our righteousness, but because of your great mercy" (9:18). The seventy weeks point to the Messiah, who would be cut off (9:26).
Daniel sees a glorious man and is strengthened: "O man greatly loved, fear not; peace be with you" (10:19).
The kings of the north and south. God knows and rules over history.
Those who are wise will shine like the stars; many will awake to everlasting life (12:2-3). The hope of resurrection in Christ.
