@Ezr
Cyrus decrees the return to Jerusalem, and God stirs hearts to go. God moves even kings to bring his people home.
The list of those who returned. Every name mattered; God brings his people back one by one.
The altar is rebuilt and the foundation laid; some weep and some shout for joy. Restoration begins at the altar, the place of sacrifice.
Enemies oppose the rebuilding and the work stops. Opposition may delay, but it cannot cancel God's purposes.
Haggai and Zechariah encourage the people to resume building. God's word restarts what fear stopped.
Darius confirms the decree and the temple is finished with joy. God turns opposition into provision.
Ezra arrives: "The good hand of his God was on him" (7:9). Ezra set his heart to study and teach God's Word.
Ezra fasts for protection instead of asking for soldiers, and God protects them. Trust in God's protection.
Ezra's prayer of confession over the people's intermarriage. He says God has given "a little reviving" (9:8). God's mercy gives hope even after failure.
The people deal with intermarriage. Under the old covenant separation was outward; in Christ, holiness is the new nature within us.
@Neh
Nehemiah weeps over Jerusalem's broken walls and prays. God's heart for restoration begins with a burden in someone's heart.
Nehemiah asks the king and receives permission and supplies. "The good hand of my God was on me" (2:8). God gives favour.
Families rebuild sections of the wall side by side. Everyone has a part in God's work.
Opposition rises, and the workers build with a sword in one hand. "Remember the Lord, who is great and awesome" (4:14).
Nehemiah stops the rich from exploiting the poor. God's heart is justice and generosity.
Enemies try to distract and frighten Nehemiah, but the wall is finished in 52 days. "This work was done by our God" (6:16).
The list of returned exiles. God keeps account of his people.
Ezra reads the law and the people weep, but are told: "Do not grieve, for the joy of the Lord is your strength" (8:10). God wanted joy, not condemnation.
The Levites' prayer recounts Israel's history: "You are a God ready to pardon, gracious and merciful" (9:17).
The people make a covenant to keep the law. Under the old covenant they promised; in the new covenant God promises and fulfils.
Volunteers settle in Jerusalem. Willing hearts fill God's city.
The wall is dedicated with great joy that could be heard far away (12:43). God's restoration brings joy.
Nehemiah confronts failures after returning. The old covenant cycle shows our need for a new heart, which God gives in Christ.
@Est
Queen Vashti refuses the king and is removed. God is preparing a place for Esther before the crisis comes.
Esther, an orphan, becomes queen, and Mordecai uncovers a plot. God places people in position ahead of time.
Haman plots to destroy the Jews. Evil plans against God's people.
Mordecai: "Who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (4:14). Esther: "If I perish, I perish."
Esther finds favour with the king and he extends the sceptre. In Christ we have favour and access to the King of kings (Hebrews 4:16).
The king cannot sleep and reads of Mordecai's loyalty. God works even through sleepless nights.
Haman is hanged on the gallows he made for Mordecai. The weapon formed against God's people turned back on the enemy.
A new decree allows the Jews to defend themselves. Jesus did not cancel the old decree; he triumphed over it (Colossians 2:14-15).
The Jews are delivered and sorrow turns to joy. God turns mourning into celebration.
Mordecai is raised to great honour, seeking the good of his people. God exalts those who serve.
@Job
Job is blameless and blessed; Satan accuses him and attacks his children and possessions. Notice who did the destroying: Satan (1:12). Jesus came to destroy the works of the devil (1 John 3:8).
Satan strikes Job with sores. Job's wife says curse God and die. The accuser attacks, but God has the final word.
Job curses the day he was born. God lets us be honest with him in our pain.
Eliphaz says the innocent never suffer. His theology sounds wise but God later rebukes it (42:7). Not all suffering is punishment.
Eliphaz urges Job to accept God's discipline. Under the new covenant Jesus bore our punishment; suffering is not God paying us back.
Job longs for comfort from his friends. "To the despairing, kindness should come from a friend" (6:14).
Job pours out his complaint. God is big enough to hear our raw honesty.
Bildad says Job's children died for their sin. A cruel conclusion; God later says the friends did not speak rightly.
Job longs for a mediator between himself and God (9:33). Jesus is that Mediator (1 Timothy 2:5).
Job questions why God made him. Job did not have the revelation of God's heart that we have in Jesus.
Zophar says Job deserves worse. Condemning theology wounds the hurting; grace comforts.
Job: "In his hand is the life of every living thing" (12:10). God is sovereign over all life.
"Though he slay me, I will hope in him" (13:15). Even in confusion, Job holds on to God.
Job wonders if a man can live again after death (14:14). Jesus answered with his resurrection.
Eliphaz speaks again with harsh accusations. Friends who accuse rather than comfort miss God's heart.
"Even now my witness is in heaven; my advocate is on high" (16:19). We have an Advocate, Jesus (1 John 2:1).
Job feels mocked and hopeless. God is near the brokenhearted.
Bildad describes the fate of the wicked. Judging others' suffering is not our place.
"I know that my Redeemer lives" (19:25). Job's faith rises to see a living Redeemer, Jesus.
Zophar says the wicked's joy is short. Job's situation was not what his friends thought.
Job observes that the wicked often prosper. Life is not as simple as reward and punishment.
Eliphaz accuses Job of hidden sins. False accusations are the accuser's tool.
Job longs to find God: "When he has tested me, I shall come out as gold" (23:10).
Job asks why God does not judge the oppressors. Justice will come in God's time.
Bildad's short speech: how can man be righteous before God? In Christ we are made righteous as a gift.
Job describes God's greatness: "These are but the outskirts of his ways" (26:14).
Job holds on to his integrity. God sees the heart.
Where can wisdom be found? "The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom" (28:28). Christ is the wisdom of God.
Job remembers the days when he helped the poor and was honoured. God remembers our good.
Job laments his present humiliation. Jesus too was mocked and despised; he understands.
Job's final defence of his integrity. Job relied on his own righteousness; we rely on Christ's.
Elihu, angry, begins to speak. Youth can see what elders miss, yet even Elihu does not see the full picture.
Elihu: God speaks to man and provides a ransom (33:24). The ransom is Jesus.
Elihu defends God's justice. God is never unjust.
Elihu: our righteousness does not benefit God. God's love toward us is free, not earned.
Elihu speaks of God's greatness in suffering. God's purposes are bigger than we see.
Elihu describes God's majesty in the storm. God speaks through his creation.
God speaks from the whirlwind: "Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?" God answers Job with his presence, not explanations.
God's care for wild animals. If he feeds the young ravens, he will care for you.
Job: "I lay my hand on my mouth", and God describes Behemoth, his mighty creature. Humility before God, who is greater than anything we fear.
God describes Leviathan. No power can stand against its Maker; Jesus has defeated the ancient serpent.
God rebukes Job's friends for not speaking rightly, Job prays for them, and God restores double (42:10). God's heart is restoration. "The Lord is full of compassion and mercy" (James 5:11).
